How Long Does NBI Clearance Take?

There is no single accurate time for every application. No-HIT branch processing can proceed quickly after biometrics, HIT review takes longer, mailed clearance has its own timeline, and eGovPH renewal may be much faster for eligible users.

By NBI Clearance Help Editorial TeamUpdated 11 October 2026
Official NBI Portal ↗

Quick answer

It depends on your situation. A no-HIT branch visit can move to printing during the same visit after biometrics; a HIT usually means 5–10 working days of manual review; mailed clearance from abroad is stated as a maximum of five working days after documents arrive; and eligible eGovPH renewal may take as little as five minutes. Queues and office conditions add real-world time on top of every figure.

Why “same day” can be misleading

A no-HIT application may proceed to printing and release after biometric capture and record checking, but your total visit time also depends on queue length, office conditions and whether your transaction is ready. A Citizen’s Charter duration for an individual service step is not the same thing as a guaranteed door-to-door appointment duration.

HIT applications

A HIT creates a separate manual-review timeline. The current NBI how-to page describes 5 to 10 working days as the usual return period, while the exact branch instruction should control your case. See the HIT status guide for what the review involves.

Applications from abroad

NBI’s mailed-clearance page says processing takes a maximum of five working days after the documents are received. Courier or international-mail transit is outside that processing figure. The applying from abroad guide covers the document side.

What affects your total time

Queues and office conditions

The biggest variable in a branch visit is the queue — the number of applicants ahead of you, the number of open counters, and any system slowdowns that day. Arriving with complete documents and a confirmed paid transaction keeps your own part short, but it cannot shorten the line.

Document readiness

Missing or mismatched IDs send applicants back to the queue or home entirely. Check the requirements guide and valid IDs list before travelling.

HIT review

A HIT pauses the normal flow for manual verification of the record match. Nothing you do at the counter shortens it; the branch's return date is the timeline to follow.

Payment and transaction readiness

An unpaid or unposted transaction can stall you before biometrics even start. Confirm the portal shows PAID — our payment troubleshooting guide covers what to do if it does not.

Planning around a deadline

If you need the clearance for a job or travel date, work backwards from that date and add buffer. A no-HIT application still needs registration, payment posting and an appointment date you can actually get — the appointment guide covers booking. For a possible HIT, the 5–10 working-day review is the figure to plan around, not the fastest case.

Do not build a non-refundable deadline around a best-case estimate. Queues, technical interruptions, a HIT, or an incomplete document can all move a correctly prepared application past the fastest figures quoted anywhere.

Prevent avoidable delay

Use contact details you control, match the profile to your IDs, save the reference before payment, wait for the correct transaction to show PAID and bring complete original documents. Check official closure notices before travelling. Do not build a non-refundable deadline around a “same-day” estimate: queues, technical interruptions or manual review can affect a correctly prepared application.

Processing-time FAQs

Can I get NBI Clearance in one day?

A no-HIT branch application can move to printing during the same visit after biometric and record checks, but queue and office conditions vary.

Does a HIT always take 10 days?

No. NBI describes a usual 5–10 working-day period; follow the exact return date from your branch.

How fast is eGovPH renewal?

NBI says eligible users may renew in as little as five minutes through eGovPH.

Does paying earlier speed up NBI processing?

No. Payment completes your transaction so you can proceed, but it does not shorten record checks, HIT review or queues. Prepare documents and arrive ready instead.

How will I know if my application gets a HIT?

The branch tells you after biometrics and record checking during your visit. If a HIT is raised, follow the return date and instructions the branch gives you.

Can I follow up on a HIT before the return date?

Follow the exact return date and instructions from your branch. Returning much earlier usually does not move the manual review forward.

Do weekends and holidays add to the waiting time?

NBI describes HIT review in working days, which excludes weekends and public holidays. Plan around that when estimating your total wait.
