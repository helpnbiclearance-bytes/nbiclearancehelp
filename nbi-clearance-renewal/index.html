How to Renew NBI Clearance in 2026

Choose the renewal route that matches your previous clearance, current details and location. In 2026, eGovPH adds a major digital renewal option for eligible verified users.

Renewal routes in 2026

Who qualifies for online renewal?

Online renewal is a conditional shortcut, not a universal right. Your previous certificate and your current personal details decide whether the shortcut is open to you. Use this table as a starting map, then confirm your own eligibility in the live portal.

Your situation Likely route
Clearance issued after 2014, personal details unchanged Online renewal with courier delivery
Verified eGovPH account with the service available eGovPH eClearance digital renewal
Outside the Philippines, clearance issued in 2014 or later Mailed renewal or renewal through a designated representative
Clearance issued before 2014 Treated as a new application
Name, civil status or address changed Follow the live NBI instructions; the quick-renewal path may not apply

If the portal says you are not qualified for online renewal, do not keep retrying the same button — read that guide to find out which alternative fits.

Eligibility is record-dependent. Two people with clearances from the same year can see different options, because the portal checks your specific account and record. Your neighbor’s experience does not guarantee yours.

eGovPH renewal

The 6 July 2026 NBI announcement says verified eGovPH users can access NBI Clearance services on the Super App. It specifically describes the ability to retrieve, renew and access a digital copy. The app’s exact buttons and eligibility rules can evolve, so use the current app flow rather than relying on old screenshots.

What eGovPH renewal can do for you

For eligible users, the appeal is obvious: no branch queue, no courier wait — NBI says the process may take as little as five minutes. That figure describes a best case for a clean, verified record, not a promise for every applicant. Records with complications can still require further steps.

Before you rely on the app

Make sure your eGovPH account is verified and the NBI service is actually visible in your app version. App interfaces change with updates, so a tutorial written for an older version may show buttons that no longer exist. The eGovPH eClearance guide tracks the current state of the service.

Online renewal and delivery

The NBI’s current how-to page says applicants with an NBI Clearance issued after 2014 and no changes to personal details can use the NBI Clearance Online Renewal option and have the clearance delivered by courier. Check the live portal because eligibility is account- and record-dependent.

How the delivery works

Instead of returning to a branch, you complete the renewal transaction online and the printed clearance is delivered to the address on your transaction. Make sure the delivery address is complete and someone can receive the package — a failed delivery can send your document back into a redelivery loop.

Delivery timing expectations

NBI does not publish a single guaranteed delivery timeframe, and courier transit varies by location. If you need the renewed clearance by a specific date, start the renewal early enough that a few extra days of transit do not cause a problem. For a sense of overall timing, see the processing time guide.

Double-check the delivery address. An old address from a previous application is a common cause of delivery failure. Update it in the transaction before paying.

Renewal fees and payment

NBI generates the exact renewal amount inside your live transaction — that figure is the one that counts. For reference, the standard published schedule is a ₱130 basic fee plus an e-payment service charge of around ₱25–₱30; the clearance fee breakdown explains the components and the available payment channels.

Delivery charges

Courier delivery can add a separate charge on top of the clearance fee. Because delivery fees depend on the courier and destination, confirm the full total — fee plus delivery — inside the transaction before you pay.

Paying for someone else’s renewal

If you are handling a renewal for a relative, pay through the official transaction tied to their application, not through a personal transfer to a stranger offering “renewal assistance.” The anti-fixer warning on every page of this site exists because renewal scams are common.

Renewal from abroad

For overseas renewals, NBI states that only clearances issued starting 2014 may be renewed through mail or a designated representative; older clearances are treated as new applications. If your address or civil status changed, NBI instructs applicants to note the change on the old clearance certificate.

Mail vs representative

The mailed-clearance route lets you send documents by post, while the representative route lets someone you authorize act on your behalf in the Philippines. Both follow the same eligibility rules — the 2014 cutoff applies either way. Read applying for NBI Clearance from abroad for the document and fingerprint details.

Start earlier than you think

International mailing adds days or weeks in each direction. If your clearance is needed for a visa or overseas employment deadline, begin the mailed renewal well in advance and use trackable mailing where possible.

How to choose the correct NBI renewal route

Do not start by assuming that every old clearance qualifies for the same online renewal. The right route depends on when the previous certificate was issued, whether personal details changed, whether your account is eligible for eGovPH eClearance, and whether you are inside or outside the Philippines.

Use eGovPH when the service is available to your verified account

The July 2026 NBI announcement is the clearest new development in renewal. Eligible verified eGovPH users can access NBI Clearance services through the Super App. If the renewal option is present for your verified account, follow the current app instructions rather than an old website screenshot.

Use the normal online renewal option when your record fits that route

The NBI how-to page says clearances issued after 2014 with no changes to personal details can use the online renewal option and courier delivery. Because this is an eligibility condition, do not assume that every certificate or every changed record can skip a branch visit.

Use the mailed-clearance process when you are abroad

NBI has separate overseas instructions. For renewal by mail or representative, the mailed-clearance page says only certificates issued starting 2014 may be renewed using that route; earlier certificates are processed as new applications.

Decision shortcut. Ask yourself three questions in order: (1) Am I abroad? → mailed route. (2) Is my eGovPH account verified with the NBI service visible? → app route. (3) Was my clearance issued after 2014 with unchanged details? → online renewal. If the answer to all three is no, plan a branch-based application.

What if your name, civil status or address changed?

Changes matter because renewal is built around an existing clearance record. The mailed-clearance instructions specifically tell overseas applicants to write changes of address or civil status on the old certificate. For any route where your personal information changed, rely on the live NBI instructions rather than forcing a quick-renewal path meant for unchanged details.

Common change scenarios

Marriage-related name changes, corrected birth details and updated addresses are the usual cases. In each one, the renewal system needs to reconcile your old record with your current identity — which is exactly the situation the unchanged-details shortcut was not designed for. Expect the process to look more like a standard application, possibly including a branch visit with your updated IDs.

Make sure your requirements are in order for the identity you now use, and allow extra time compared with a straightforward unchanged-details renewal.

When renewal is not available: apply as new

Some situations simply do not qualify for any renewal shortcut. The clearest case is a clearance issued before 2014 — NBI treats it as a new application, including on the mailed-clearance route. A portal message saying you are not qualified for online renewal is the system telling you the same thing.

In that case, follow the complete application guide from the beginning: register, complete your profile, book an NBI appointment and visit the branch with your documents. It takes longer than a renewal, but it is the correct and only official path for your situation.

Do not fight the eligibility result. Repeatedly retrying a renewal button you do not qualify for wastes time. The new-application route is well documented and gets you a valid clearance just the same.

How early should you renew?

There is no official “renew X days before expiry” rule published by NBI, so the right timing depends on why you need the clearance. Work backward from your deadline instead of forward from today.

If an employer or agency asked for it

Start as soon as the request lands. Between eligibility checks, payment, possible HIT review and courier transit, a renewal can still take one to three weeks in unlucky cases. Beginning early turns all of that into background noise.

If your clearance is simply aging

Clearances are generally treated as valid for one year, but many requesting parties prefer one issued within the last six months. If yours is approaching that informal six-month mark and you expect to need it soon, renewing now is cheaper than rushing later.

NBI Clearance renewal FAQs

Can I renew NBI Clearance entirely through eGovPH?

Eligible verified users can use the eGovPH eClearance service. The exact options visible to you depend on the current app and your account eligibility.

How fast is eGovPH renewal?

NBI says eligible users may renew in as little as five minutes. That is not a guarantee for every record.

How do I know if I qualify for online renewal?

The NBI how-to page describes online renewal for clearances issued after 2014 with no changes to personal details. The live portal confirms eligibility for your specific account and record.

Can a post-2014 clearance be renewed online?

The current NBI how-to page says an online renewal route is available for certain post-2014 clearances when personal details have not changed.

What if my old clearance was issued before 2014?

NBI treats pre-2014 clearances as new applications, including for the mailed-clearance route. Follow the standard application process instead of the renewal flow.

Do I need to visit a branch to renew?

Not always. Eligible eGovPH and online renewals can avoid a branch visit, but renewals that do not qualify follow the normal branch process.

How will I receive my renewed clearance?

Online renewals can use courier delivery to your address. eGovPH eClearance provides a digital copy through the app. Branch-processed renewals are released at the branch.

Can I renew from abroad?

Yes, using the official mailed-clearance procedure if your certificate qualifies. NBI says clearances issued before 2014 are treated as new applications for that route.

What if my details changed since my last clearance?

Changes to name, civil status or address can disqualify the quick-renewal path. Follow the live NBI instructions for your situation rather than forcing a renewal route meant for unchanged records.
