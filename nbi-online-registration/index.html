NBI Online Registration: Create Your Account

Start only on the official NBI Clearance Application Portal. Use an active email address and mobile number, complete OTP verification and match your profile to your IDs.

By NBI Clearance Help Editorial TeamUpdated 11 October 2026
Official NBI Portal ↗

Registration steps

  1. Open the official NBI Clearance Application Portal. Type the address yourself or use the button above — never register through a link forwarded in a chat group. The only legitimate starting point is clearance.nbi.gov.ph.
  2. Complete the Register section if you do not already have an account. If you registered before — even years ago — try the NBI clearance login and account recovery first instead of creating a second profile.
  3. Use an active mobile number and email address so you can receive the OTP and recover the account if needed. Both channels matter: the OTP may arrive by either, and recovery depends on reaching at least one of them later.
  4. Log in and complete the Applicant Information Form. This is the profile the NBI will match against your IDs, so accuracy here prevents problems at the branch.
  5. Review the spelling of names, birth details and other information before saving. Read every field twice. Fixing a typo now takes seconds; fixing it after saving can mean a branch visit.

Before you register: what to prepare

Registration itself takes a few minutes, but gathering these beforehand prevents the most common stalls:

  • An active email address you control. Not a borrowed address, not one you check twice a year — you need it for verification and recovery.
  • A mobile number with good signal. The OTP is time-sensitive; registering from a dead spot is asking for trouble.
  • Your valid IDs within reach. You will enter identity details that must match them — see the valid IDs guide and the requirements guide.
  • A stable internet connection. Dropped sessions mid-form are how half-finished duplicate profiles are born.
  • Ten unhurried minutes. Rushing through the Applicant Information Form is how name misspellings happen.
One sitting is best. Start registration when you can finish it — an abandoned half-profile is worse than no profile, because you may forget it exists and create a conflicting second one later.

The OTP verification step

The one-time password (OTP) is how the portal confirms that the email address and mobile number you entered actually belong to you. The current how-to page says an OTP is used to verify registration, so access to those contact details matters.

When the code arrives, enter it promptly — OTPs are short-lived by design. Enter the most recent code if more than one arrived, and do not request code after code in rapid succession: only the latest usually remains usable, and too many requests can trigger a temporary rate limit.

Never share your OTP

No legitimate NBI staff member, “agent,” or helper will ever ask for your OTP. Anyone who has your code can take over the verification — and with it, the account. If someone is pressuring you for the code, stop the process and start over on your own.

First-Time Job Seekers should use the dedicated facility

If you are claiming the free NBI Clearance benefit under the First-Time Job Seeker program, the NBI’s current how-to page directs you to the dedicated FTJS portal rather than treating the application as a normal registration.

This is easy to get wrong: the normal portal will happily let you register and pay, and you will have paid for something you could have gotten free. Check your eligibility before you register — the First-Time Job Seeker guide explains who qualifies and how the dedicated process works.

What information should match your IDs?

Pay particular attention to your legal name, birth information and other identifying fields. If the online profile contains a different spelling from the ID you bring, the branch may need to resolve the discrepancy before printing the clearance.

The matching rule is simple: the profile should read like your IDs do. Use your full legal name in the order your IDs show it, double-check birth dates digit by digit, and do not “simplify” anything — no dropped middle names, no nicknames, no missing suffixes. What feels like a small shortcut online becomes a discrepancy interview at the branch.

Keep control of the email and mobile number

The registration process uses an OTP and the account may need recovery later. Avoid using a temporary number or an email address you cannot access reliably.

Do not create duplicate accounts as a first fix

If the problem is a forgotten password or OTP issue, use the official account recovery route first. Repeated accounts can make it harder to remember which profile contains the paid transaction.

Review the profile before saving

Saving is the point of no return for casual edits. Walk through this check field by field:

FieldQuality check
Full legal nameUse spelling and sequence supported by your IDs; do not casually omit a suffix or middle name.
Birth detailsCheck the date and place carefully because they help distinguish records.
Email and mobile numberUse contact details you control for OTP and account recovery.
Address and civil informationEnter truthful, current information in the requested format.
Selected valid IDChoose an ID you can bring while preparing the two IDs required for a standard visit.
The two-minute rule: after filling the form, stand up, get water, come back, and re-read everything once with fresh eyes before saving. Most typos are caught on the second read, not the first.

If the OTP does not arrive

Confirm the phone number or email, then check spam folders, message filtering and mobile signal. Avoid requesting many codes rapidly because only the latest may remain usable and a temporary rate limit may apply. Never ask a stranger to receive the OTP: control of the verification channel can become control of the account.

Work through it methodically: first verify you typed the number and email correctly (one wrong digit is the most common cause), then check spam/junk folders and SMS spam filtering, then move to an area with better signal and request one fresh code. If codes consistently fail across both channels, the problem is usually the contact details themselves — and the fix is correcting them, not requesting a twentieth code. The OTP-not-received guide covers this in more depth.

Recover before duplicating. If an account may already exist, try the official recovery options before creating a second profile with conflicting information.

Account recovery and login problems

Already registered but can’t get in? The order of operations matters:

  1. Use the official “forgot password” / recovery route on the portal login page with your registered email or mobile number.
  2. Check both contact channels — the recovery message may land in spam or on the secondary channel.
  3. Do not create a new account yet. A second profile with slightly different details is how people end up paying on the wrong transaction.
  4. Still stuck? Use official NBI help channels rather than third-party “account fixers.”

The NBI clearance login guide walks through the sign-in and recovery options. If you suspect someone else accessed your account — an OTP you did not request, details you did not change — treat it as urgent: recover the account through official channels immediately and do not share access with anyone “helping.”

Stick to one account per applicant

One person, one NBI online account. Duplicates cause a specific, painful problem: you pay on profile A, then show up at the branch logged in as profile B, and nothing matches. Staff then have to untangle which profile owns the paid transaction — if it can be untangled at all that day.

Not sure whether you already have an account? Search your email for past NBI messages and try the login/recovery route before registering fresh. If you find an old account, revive it — an old profile with your correct details is always better than a shiny new conflicting one. And once you are in, the next step is booking: see the NBI appointment guide and the complete application walkthrough. If anything else goes wrong along the way, the Help Center collects the fixes in one place.

Registration FAQs

Where should I register?

Use the official NBI Clearance Application Portal at clearance.nbi.gov.ph.

Why does NBI need an active mobile number and email?

The current how-to page says an OTP is used to verify registration, so access to those contact details matters.

Should first-time job seekers register on the normal page?

If you are claiming the FTJS benefit, the NBI directs you to the dedicated First-Time Job Seeker portal.

Is NBI online registration free?

Creating the account itself is free. The clearance fee applies when you generate a paid transaction for the clearance itself.

Can I register with a foreign mobile number?

The process centers on OTP delivery, so use a number you control that reliably receives messages. A number that cannot receive the OTP will block verification.

What if I lost access to my registered email?

Try the official account recovery options first. If recovery is not possible, use official help channels rather than creating a conflicting duplicate profile.

Can a relative register on my behalf?

The profile must match your own IDs, and the OTP and account control should stay with you. Letting someone else own your account creates identity and recovery risks.

Why does my profile name differ from my ID?

Fix spelling before saving the profile. After saving, a mismatch between the profile and the ID you bring may need to be resolved at the branch.

How do I delete my NBI online account?

The guidance reviewed does not document a self-service account deletion. For account removal requests, contact NBI through official help channels.
