How to Pay NBI Clearance via GCash

GCash is one of the payment methods named by current NBI guidance. The important part is to create the NBI transaction and reference number first, then pay through the supported biller flow.

By NBI Clearance Help Editorial TeamUpdated 11 October 2026
Official NBI Portal ↗

Quick answer

NBI transaction first, GCash second. Generate the reference number in the official NBI portal, open GCash bill payment, choose the NBI government biller, enter the exact reference number and total, confirm every field, then keep the GCash receipt and recheck the NBI portal until the status shows PAID.

Before opening GCash

  1. Finish the NBI online application far enough for the system to generate a reference number.
  2. Save or screenshot that reference number.
  3. Confirm the amount shown by the official NBI system.
  4. Make sure your GCash wallet has enough balance for the total, including the service charge.
  5. Check that you are logged in to your own GCash account on your own device.
Why the order matters. The reference number is what connects your GCash payment to your NBI application. Paying first and generating the transaction later leaves the money with nowhere to attach. See the full NBI Clearance payment guide for how the reference system works.

GCash payment flow

In the current GCash experience, NBI payments are commonly handled through the app’s bill-payment flow under government billers. Because app menus can change, use the biller name and fields shown in your current GCash app and match the NBI reference number exactly.

Finding the NBI biller in the app

Open the bill-payment or "Pay Bills" section and look under government billers for the NBI option. If a search box is available, typing "NBI" is usually faster than scrolling. Menu names and layouts change with app updates, so do not rely on screenshots from old tutorials — follow the labels in the version installed on your phone.

What the confirmation screen should show

Before you approve, the confirmation screen should display the biller name (NBI), the reference number you entered, and the total amount. Read all three. If any field looks wrong, go back and fix it rather than approving and hoping — a payment sent to the wrong reference is much harder to sort out afterwards.

Do not send money to a person. NBI payment should be attached to your official reference number. A personal GCash number promising appointment help or a faster HIT clearance is not the normal official payment route.

After paying

  • Keep the GCash receipt.
  • Return to the official NBI portal and check the transaction status.
  • If the payment is delayed, do not make a second payment until you have verified the first transaction.
  • Save the GCash transaction ID, date and time alongside the NBI reference number.

GCash mistakes that can cause payment trouble

Using the wrong reference number

The payment is tied to the NBI transaction identifier. Check every digit before confirming the bill payment.

Paying before the NBI transaction exists

Generate the reference number on the official NBI portal first. A payment without the correct transaction context cannot safely be matched to your application.

Paying a personal GCash number

A person asking you to transfer money directly to their wallet is not the normal official NBI bill-payment route. Do not pay a “rush fee” for a guaranteed slot or cleared HIT.

Closing the app before the receipt appears

Wait for the GCash confirmation and receipt before leaving the payment screen. If the app crashes mid-payment, check your transaction history first — the payment may have gone through even without a receipt on screen.

App or network problems during payment

If GCash freezes or your connection drops during payment, do not immediately try again. Open your transaction history first: if the amount was deducted, treat it as a completed payment and follow the pending-payment steps. Only retry when the history clearly shows no deduction.

Biller or payeeUse the NBI option in the supported flow, not a personal number sent in chat.

If GCash confirms but NBI does not

Save the receipt and transaction ID, then compare its reference with the NBI transaction. Recheck the official portal rather than relying only on an SMS. If the status remains unresolved, contact GCash and official NBI support with the date, amount, masked account information and reference. Do not share an OTP, MPIN, wallet balance or unrelated transaction history.

Do not immediately pay twice. First establish whether the original payment completed, failed or was reversed.

If you need to log back in to the NBI portal to recheck the status and run into access problems, the login guide covers the common fixes.

GCash payment FAQs

How much should I expect to pay?

The NBI currently lists a ₱130 basic fee plus an e-payment service charge usually around ₱25–₱30. Confirm the live total shown for your transaction before payment.

What should I save after paying?

Save the GCash receipt and your NBI reference number until the transaction is reflected and the branch processing is complete.

Should I pay again if the status is slow?

No, not as your first step. Verify the first transaction and use official support if it remains unresolved.

Where do I find NBI in the GCash app?

NBI payments are commonly handled through the GCash bill-payment flow under government billers. App menus change, so search for the NBI biller in your current app and match the reference number exactly.

GCash deducted money but shows the transaction as failed — what now?

Check your GCash transaction history to confirm whether the money left your wallet, save any receipt or transaction ID, and do not pay again until you know the outcome. If the amount was debited, contact GCash support and official NBI support with the reference number and proof.

Can I use my GCash to pay for someone else's NBI transaction?

The payment is tied to the reference number of the applicant's own NBI transaction. If you help someone pay, use their exact reference number and amount, and never ask for or share their portal password, OTP or MPIN.

Does GCash add its own charge on top of the NBI fee?

E-payment channels usually add a service charge — the current guidance mentions around ₱25–₱30 for NBI transactions. The exact live total appears in the official NBI transaction before you confirm, so read it there.
